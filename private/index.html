PRIVATE

A z/halve payout is a claim. The follower proves they hold a leaf in their tier's tree for the round, without saying which leaf, and the program pays USDC on Solana to an address they choose. The amount and the tier are public. The link between the claim and the handle is what the proof hides, among the members of that tier for that round.

Plain text of this page: /text/private.txt

This page describes what the programs do. The client that makes secrets and proofs is not built. The relayer is not built. Nothing is deployed or audited.

§1

THE CLAIM, STEP BY STEP

  1. Make a secret. 31 random bytes, made on your own device, never shared. Keep a copy somewhere safe. If you lose it, you lose every claim it would have made. Nobody can recover it.
  2. Make the commitment. commitment = Poseidon(1, secret). It is safe to publish. A weak secret is not: if the secret can be guessed, anyone can link your claims to your handle.
  3. Bind it. Post the commitment from your X handle. The attestor checks the post and writes the commitment onchain beside your roster entry, with the post id. From then on anyone can see that your handle has this commitment.
  4. Get registered. During each era, anyone can register you into that era's round. The program checks you are on the roster, reads your commitment, and reads your tier from your bound wallet's balance at that moment. It adds leaf = Poseidon(commitment, tier) to your tier's tree. One registration per handle per round.
  5. The era ends. advance moves the era's USDC to zhalve_claims.
  6. The round opens. Removed followers' leaves are zeroed first. Then fund_era fixes which tiers open (at least 20 live members each) and what one claim in each tier pays. These amounts do not change for the round.
  7. Make the proof. From the public registration records, rebuild your tier's tree and your leaf's path. Compute nullifier = Poseidon(2, secret, round). Pick a fresh recipient address, a relayer and a fee. The proof binds all of them.
  8. Submit. Hand the proof to a relayer, which pays the transaction fee and takes its fee from the claim. Submitting it yourself works too, but your wallet then appears in the transaction.
  9. The program checks and pays. It checks the round is open, the tier is open, the nullifier is new and canonical, the fee is at most 5%, the destination is the recipient's own USDC account, the proof verifies against the tree's current root, and the claim fits in the pool. Then it records the nullifier and pays the tier amount less the fee to the recipient, and the fee to the relayer.
  10. The window closes. Claims are open for one era. After that, whatever is left is sold and burned. An unclaimed share is gone.

§2

BEFORE YOU CLAIM

The proof hides which member of your tier you are. Everything around the proof can still give you away. None of this is enforced by the programs.

ruledetail
networkuse Tor or a VPN when you talk to a relayer. a relayer sees where the request came from
fresh addressclaim to a new address each round. a reused address links your claims
waitdo not move the funds right after claiming. claims and transfers close together in time are easy to match
round amountswhen you move funds on, move round amounts, not the exact claim amount. the claim amount is the same for everyone in your tier, but a later transfer of that exact sum stands out
gasa fresh address has no SOL. funding it from your own wallet links them
small tiersa tier with 20 members hides you among 20. that is weak. a tier near the minimum is weak privacy even when it opens
timingclaiming as the only claim in a quiet hour narrows the set
self-submissionsubmitting your own claim shows your wallet
§3

OPTIONAL EXIT TO SHIELDED ZEC (UNTESTED)

A follower can move claimed USDC from Solana to shielded ZEC through NEAR Intents. This is outside the programs and nobody has tested it for z/halve. Sources disagree about which ZEC addresses NEAR Intents can pay: the SwapKit docs say NEAR Intents cannot quote unified addresses, and other sources say it can. Check with a small amount first. NEAR Intents and its solvers see the destination and the amount. ZEC carries delisting risk: exchanges have delisted privacy coins before.
