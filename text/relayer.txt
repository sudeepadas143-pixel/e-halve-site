RELAYER
-------
Not built. This page is a design. There is no relayer code, no endpoint and no operator.

A relayer submits a claim for a holder so the holder's own wallet never appears in the transaction. It pays the Solana fees and the rent for the accounts the claim creates, and takes a fee out of the claim. The program binds the relayer's address and the fee into the proof, so a relayer cannot change who is paid or how much it takes.

WHAT IT WOULD DO
----------------
1. Receive a proof and its public inputs from a holder over HTTPS, reachable over Tor.
2. Check the proof locally against the verifying key and the tree's current root. Reject it if the root has moved, so the holder can rebuild.
3. Check the fee covers its costs and is at most 5% of the tier amount.
4. Build one transaction: create the recipient's and its own USDC accounts if they do not exist, then call claim.
5. Sign as fee payer and submit. Report the signature back.

rule              detail
pays              transaction fees, the nullifier account rent (981,360 lamports), and the
                  rent for any USDC account it creates (2,039,280 lamports each)
earns             the fee bound in the proof, at most 5% of the claim (config guess)
cannot            change the recipient, the relayer or the fee. claim twice. redirect funds
can               refuse a claim, delay it, log the request's network origin and the
                  recipient, and see when the request arrived
copying           anyone who copies a submitted claim pays the same recipient and the same
                  relayer. the nullifier stops a second payment
stale root        if a removal changes the root first, the claim fails. the holder rebuilds

WHAT IT LEAKS
-------------
A relayer sees the network origin of each request, the recipient, the tier and the time. If it logs them and later learns a holder's IP, it can link that holder to a claim. Tor or a VPN reduces this. Several independent relayers reduce it further. With one relayer, it sees every claim.
